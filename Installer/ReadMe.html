CopyCatch

Version __VERSION__ · for macOS 13 Ventura or later · Intel and Apple Silicon

CopyCatch keeps a history of everything you copy, and it can read the text in pictures, screenshots and PDFs so you can copy and paste it. It all happens on your Mac: nothing you copy is ever sent anywhere.

Install

  1. Drag CopyCatch onto the Applications folder in this window.
  2. Open Applications and double-click CopyCatch.

The first time you open it

CopyCatch isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS will say it can't check the app for malicious software. This only happens once:

  1. Click Done (or OK) on the warning.
  2. Go to System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to the CopyCatch message.
  3. Open CopyCatch again and click Open to confirm.

Two more permissions you may be asked for:

  • Screen Recording, for Capture Text from Screen (⌘⇧2): System Settings › Privacy & Security › Screen & System Audio Recording › turn on CopyCatch, then reopen the app.
  • Reading what other apps copy, on newer versions of macOS: choose Allow.

What it does

Clipboard historyEverything you copy appears at the top of the list. Copying the same thing again moves it back to the top.
Read text from imagesDrag images or PDFs onto the window, press ⌘O to open files, press ⌘⇧2 and drag over part of the screen, or simply copy an image. The text it finds is saved in your history.
CategoriesGroups the list using keywords you choose, for example Links, Tickets, Commands or Emails.
Select several itemsClick one, ⇧-click to select a range, ⌘-click to add or remove single items. Then copy, delete or right-click them all at once.
CopyDouble-click an item, press ⌘C, or use the Copy button. Several items are joined together with the separator you choose in Settings.
Lowercase⌘⇧L copies the text in lowercase without changing the saved item.
PinKeeps an item at the top and stops it being removed automatically.
RenameRight-click › Rename… to give an item a title. Titles count for category matching too.
SearchThe search field filters by title and text.
Menu barClick the butterfly-net icon for quick access to your latest items. ⌘1 to ⌘9 copy the first nine.

Settings (⌘,)

  • General: turn recording on or off, read text from copied images, how many items to keep (25 to 1000), how long items can stay (forever, or 1 hour up to 90 days), the separator for copying several items, and a button to clear all unpinned items.
  • Categories: give each category a name and some comma-separated keywords. An item goes into the first category whose keywords appear in its title or text. Drag categories to change which one wins.
  • Appearance: the list font and size, with a live preview, and whether to show the menu bar icon and how many items its menu shows.

Your privacy

  • Your history is stored only on this Mac, in ~/Library/Application Support/CopyCatch. To wipe it, use Clear All Unpinned Items… in Settings › General, or quit CopyCatch and delete that folder.
  • Passwords copied from password managers that mark them as private are never saved.
  • On a work Mac, check your company's IT policy first: a clipboard history keeps whatever you copy.

Uninstall

Quit CopyCatch, drag it from Applications to the Bin, and delete the ~/Library/Application Support/CopyCatch folder.
